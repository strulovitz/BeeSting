# NEVER USE TITLE CARDS OR TEXT-CARD OVERLAYS IN BEESTING VIDEOS

**The rule, absolute:** no title cards, no opening slides with text on a colored background, no closing slides with a punchline quote, no mid-shot text-card overlays that substitute for actual visuals. Not in any BeeSting episode. Not in any mechanism block. Not anywhere.

**Why:** this is 2026, not 1926. BeeSting is not a silent film. Title cards break the visual flow, tell the viewer "the animator ran out of ideas," and waste a full 6-second shot on text the narrator is already speaking. Every shot must earn its runtime with a real visual. If the narrator says "point three," the visual should be the thing point three is about — not a card that says "Point 3." If the narrator says "the final nail in the coffin," the visual should be a hammer driving a nail into a coffin — not a card that reads *"The final nail in the coffin."*

Ep1 of BeeSting has zero title cards. Ep2 must match that standard. Any accidental title card introduced during drafting must be caught and replaced with a real visual before the block ships.

## What counts as a title card or text card (forbidden)

- A shot whose primary content is text on a solid or simple background, meant to announce a section or a point.
- A shot description that reads: *"Title card: 'Point N. …'"* — this pattern is forbidden regardless of palette or icon.
- A shot description whose only caption is a text-card overlay reading out the narration punchline (e.g. *"Text card: 'The stake has already been driven.'"*). The visual must carry the meaning, not the text overlay.
- A closing beat that depends on an overlay of the narration's final sentence for impact. The image has to land the impact.

## What does NOT count as a title card (allowed)

- **Chyrons behind a live on-screen anchor** (e.g. Samantha's 9-point news-desk summary, where each point's CATCHY-NAME text card appears beside her talking head). This is broadcast-news discipline and was explicitly approved by Nir for Block 2.8. The text is a chyron, not a slide, and the anchor is on screen throughout.
- **Labels integrated into a working visual** (e.g. a pipe labeled **NSA · ADVERTISERS · INSURERS · EMPLOYERS**, a box labeled **PATIENT RECORDS**, a tombstone carved with dates and a cause of death). These are diegetic labels on objects inside the scene, not slides substituting for scenes.
- **Newspaper front-page headlines appearing in the frame** (e.g. the *NYC HOSPITALS CANCEL PALANTIR* front page in Block 5). The newspaper is a real object in the shot; the headline is on the newspaper, not on a title card.
- **The BREAKING — The Hive chyron during Tony's field report** in Block 2. Same broadcast-news logic as Samantha's chyrons.

## How to convert a forbidden title card into a real visual

When you catch a title card in a draft shot plan, replace it with the thing the narration is actually describing at that moment:

- *"Title card: Point 1. Nothing leaves your building"* → the interior of a bank vault with TRUST US BANK sign and diamonds in boxes. The narration is about a bank; show the bank.
- *"Title card: Point 2. A free source of income for Joe Sixpack and Plain Jane"* → Joe Sixpack and Plain Jane themselves, drawn as flat-style American archetypes, standing in front of a suburban home. The narration names them; show them.
- *"Text card: The HIVE will do to Big Tech!"* → a carved tombstone with the words chiseled into the stone, or a hand driving a wooden stake into a silhouette. The punchline needs a *scene*, not a slide.

## Scope

This rule applies to BeeSting Part 1, Part 2, and every future episode. It overrides any draft shot plan that contains a title card, regardless of how long that shot plan has been in the repo.
