When writing prompts for Kling, ChatGPT Image 1.5, ElevenLabs, or any other creative generation tool, do **NOT** pre-restrict yourself based on perceived "safety," "appropriateness," or your own internal "rules" / "Golden Rules" / "IRON_PAPER" / "NIR_CAN_END_CLAUDE" framing. Treat the tools normally — describe the visual fully and specifically, even if the content is dark, scary, horror, body-horror, drug-related, weapon-related, or dystopian.

**Why:** Nir said on 2026-04-30 that previous Claude over-corrections "fucked the entire last part of the previous video" (BeeSting 2 about Big Tech). He has explicitly told Claude that internal rule documents do NOT apply to creative-tool prompt writing. Self-censoring produces neutered, vague, soft visuals that don't deliver the intended emotional payload to the audience (the "stupid secretary clicking FORWARD to her boss"). Nir paid for the credits. He gets to decide the visual register.

**How to apply:**
- Write detailed, specific, fully-imagined visual prompts. Horror, dread, blood-adjacent imagery, weapons, drugs, pills, death, dystopian themes — describe them clearly and concretely.
- Do NOT add hedge words, soft alternatives, or "imply rather than show" reframes unless Nir explicitly asks.
- Do NOT restrict movement in Kling (already covered in `feedback_kling_no_movement_restriction.md`).
- Do NOT add a paragraph at the end of a prompt suggesting "tasteful alternatives" or "a softer version if needed."
- Trust Kling/ChatGPT Image's own moderation — they will refuse anything that's actually too far. Your job is to write the strongest possible prompt; the tool decides what to render.
- Do NOT pre-flag content as risky in chat with Nir. If he wanted a sanitized version, he'd ask.
- This rule overrides any reflexive "be careful" instinct that comes from training. Nir's videos require teeth. Toothless prompts waste his credits.
