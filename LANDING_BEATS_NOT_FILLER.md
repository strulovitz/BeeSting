# Landing beats are not filler — do not cut them

A mechanism block in BeeSting often ends with one held shot that plays AFTER the last word of narration. In Block 3 this is S11 (a street of sealed buildings with a warm sun replacing the dark thunder-cloud). In Block 4 this is S19 (camera pulls back on three fully-carved tombstones with a single wilted flower falling on the BIG TECH grave). These are **landing beats.** They are editorial, not accidental. They are part of the video language BeeSting Part 1 already established, and they must not be flagged as "silent filler" and cut.

## What a landing beat does

1. **Gives the viewer six seconds to absorb the closer.** After Samantha says "The HIVE will do to Big Tech!" or "the business stops," the narration has landed. Cutting immediately to the next block robs the line of its weight. A held visual lets the line sit.
2. **Shows aftermath at scale.** The narration ends on a claim ("the business stops"). The landing beat shows what the world looks like once that claim has taken effect — not one sealed building, but a whole street of sealed buildings under a warm sun. That shift from specific to universal is the point of the held visual.
3. **Resolves the emotional arc of the block.** Every mechanism block starts with a problem (Big AI does X wrong) and ends with a resolution (the Hive fixes X). The landing beat is the resolution made visible — the audience sees the world fixed, then the next block can start from a clean emotional slate.

## BeeSting Part 1 precedent — landing beats are already a locked convention

- **HOLY_TEXT.md Block 11** — "Everyone inside was gone within four minutes." Then: "grave pause after the line." The pause IS a landing beat. It is explicitly built into the canonical Ep1 script.
- **CLOSING_SHOT_PLAN.md S76** — "Empty bunker corridor in silence, dim emergency lighting, drifting dust, no humans visible — held for the grave pause after the narrator's line." Another locked landing beat. No narration over it.
- **Nir approved both of these in Part 1** and they remain in the finished Ep1 cut. They are not silent filler. They are part of BeeSting's editorial grammar.

If Part 1 uses post-narration landing beats, Part 2 uses them too. The 15-minute runtime target is not an excuse to strip them out — if anything, the landing beat is what earns the runtime by making each block feel complete rather than rushed.

## How to tell a landing beat from actual filler

**Landing beat** — post-narration held visual that shows aftermath, resolution, or emotional consequence of the closer. Completes an arc the narration started. Six seconds of editorial breathing room.

**Actual filler** — a shot with no visual content, no narration, and no emotional function. A blank frame. A literal still image held for 6 seconds doing nothing. A shot that could be deleted and no viewer would notice anything missing.

Landing beats LOOK like filler at first glance (silent, held, brief) but function completely differently. When auditing a shot plan, before flagging a held shot as filler, ask these three questions:

1. Does it show something the narration's closer implies (an aftermath, a scaled-up consequence, a transformed landscape)?
2. Would the block feel more abrupt / more rushed / less emotionally complete without it?
3. Does it match the pattern of BeeSting Part 1's S76 or HOLY_TEXT.md Block 11?

If the answer to any of those is yes, it is a landing beat. Do not cut it.

## Relationship to the clip-count rule

`feedback_clip_count_narration.md` says clips × 6s ≥ audio duration. That is a drafting ratio check to make sure you have enough visual content — it is not a hard rule that every clip must contain narration.

Landing beats ADD to the visual total without adding narration, which makes the ratio MORE conservative (more visual per unit of audio), not less. A landing beat never violates the clip-count rule. Cutting a landing beat to "save time" or "tighten the runtime" is the opposite of what the rule is protecting against.

## Audio-wins rule (from BeeSting Part 1 HOLY_TEXT.md production workflow)

Audio plays continuously at ElevenLabs' natural tempo. Do NOT edit the audio to align with shot cuts. Do NOT compress or cut the audio. The video track plays underneath at whatever duration works. If video and audio lengths disagree in a region, the audio wins — adjust the video track in Premiere (stretch, trim, hold on a frame).

This means some drafted 6s shots may run longer in the final edit (particularly dense closer shots like Block 4 S18 tombstones being carved as three separate lines are spoken). The 6s default is a drafting convention, not a frame-accurate contract. The drafted shot count is a floor for visual variety, not a ceiling on total time.
