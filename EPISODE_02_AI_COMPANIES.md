## Strategic principles (episode-specific, on top of the series bible)

Episode 1 targeted defense audiences with a military-hardware comparison ($1,600 drone vs. $32M Reaper). Episode 2 targets the tech-economics audience — anyone who has paid $20 or $200 for a ChatGPT or Claude subscription. That is a larger audience than the defense audience by at least an order of magnitude. The hook that belongs in the first five words of the title must therefore be a **money** hook, not a defense hook.

The secondary move: the chapter's strongest conceptual payload is the **Attention Deficit of Centralized AI** and the **AlphaGo Move 37** story. That story is the rare concept in the book that a non-technical viewer can internalize in 30 seconds and then not un-see. It is the chapter's equivalent of Episode 1's "Mechanism X." Lead the visual portion with it.

The tertiary move: the chapter's most specific case study is **OpenClaw / Steinberger / Anthropic / OpenAI**. That is already a public story the audience remembers, and it proves the book's "pay the inventor" thesis on camera using a real and recent name. Use it as the closer before the equation.

## Narration block structure (mirrors `HOLY_TEXT.md` approach — ~11 to 13 min total)

**Block 1 — Cold open (~20s):** The stack of receipts. On screen: ChatGPT Plus $20, ChatGPT Pro $200, Claude $20, Gemini $20, Grok $20. Narrator: *"Twenty, twenty, twenty. Monthly. Every month. The industry collects fifty-five billion dollars a year on this. And it is not profitable."* Cut to $8B compute-cost card. Then: *"One assumption supports all of this. That assumption is already wrong."*

**Block 2 — The price table (~60s):** DeepSeek vs GPT-4o side by side. Qwen vs Claude. "Ninety-six percent cheaper." "Ten to seventeen times cheaper." All three open-weight. All three free to self-host. Pause on the final row: **local hierarchical hive = $0.00 / $0.00**.

**Block 3 — What hierarchical hives add (~90s):** Brief sequel recap — DwarfQueen splits, Workers parallelize, GiantQueen combines, RajaBee coordinates. Stacking free DeepSeek / Qwen / Kimi inside the hive architecture = unlimited scaling on free hardware.

**Block 4 — Mechanism I (Attention Deficit + Move 37), the Kurzgesagt centerpiece (~3 min):** AlphaGo Move 37 visual. Lee Sedol leaves the room for 15 minutes. 1-in-10,000. The centralized AI must skim and prune. Our system does not. Every Worker gets full attention on its branch. *"No Move 37 is ever missed, because no branch is ever pruned."*

**Block 5 — Mechanism II (Scaffolding + Customization, ~90s):** Fine-tuning cost comparison ($25 per 1M tokens at OpenAI vs. electricity at home). bartowski / Unsloth / mradermacher as the existing hobbyist ecosystem. *"Train once, copy forever."* Each Worker is a fine-tuned specialist; the hive is a fleet of specialists.

**Block 6 — The OpenClaw closer (~2 min):** The Steinberger story as it happened — ClawdBot → rebrand → OAuth cut → feature absorption → April 4 2026 ban → OpenAI hires him in February 2026. On-screen: *"The software was free. The insurance policy was not."* Land on the chapter's punch: the next Steinberger has already built something orders of magnitude larger, and the offer is the same.

**Block 7 — Death spiral (~45s):** Ads arriving in ChatGPT. Criteo partnership. $200K advertiser minimums. $1B projected ad revenue against $8B annual compute cost. The arithmetic does not work.

**Block 8 — The replacement (~90s):** DeepSeek R1 alone cost Nvidia $600B in a single day — the largest single-day loss in US stock market history. That was one model, one afternoon. Now stack every Chinese model inside a hierarchical hive that runs across 1.4 billion Chinese household computers plus Chinese data centers. The world's largest AI compute base, running for free on electricity Chinese citizens already pay, coordinated by software that anyone on Earth can download today. The world used to pay OpenAI and Anthropic for AI. The world now pays DeepSeek, Alibaba, and Moonshot — the Chinese stack is cheaper, equally capable on most tasks, and not locked behind one American company's API that Anthropic or OpenAI can change at any time. America does not lose its AI lead slowly over a decade. America is replaced. The replacement starts the day a single Chinese engineer stitches DeepSeek + the hive together for mass distribution — and that engineer is not blocked by anything any American institution controls.

**Block 9 — Closing equations (same cadence as Episode 1, sharper verbs):** *"America's AI leadership equals your call. Do nothing — China rules. Forward this — America fights back."*

**Block 10 — End card (silent):** `YouTube: Nir Strulovitz` + `github.com/strulovitz`. ~5s. Final note. Cut to black.

**Episode 2 palette:** deep indigo + gold + cream. Rationale: indigo reads as "money / finance / serious economics" in the Western visual vocabulary (Economist, FT, Bloomberg) without crossing into corporate-boring blue. ## Do-not-do list

- ❌ Do not name Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Meta, or xAI in the title. Same rule as Episode 1 — brands go in the description and pinned comment, not the hook.
- ❌ Do not spoken-only-compare benchmarks. The 96% and 10-17× numbers are the single most visceral claim of the episode; they must be **visible** on screen for at least 3 seconds each, not just narrated.
- ❌ Do not reuse the Episode 1 navy palette. Viewers who watched the drone video should feel the subject change from the first frame.
- ❌ Do not animate the Move 37 Go board with moving hands placing stones. Kling writes gibberish on stone faces and stone motion at small scale looks like glitch. Pre-render board states as static images; show them in sequence with **camera motion**, not on-screen handwriting.
- ❌ Do not film the OpenClaw story as a single long sequence. Break it into four panels matching the four Anthropic moves. Each panel is a separate 6s clip so the audience registers "four steps" viscerally.
- ❌ Do not let the narration say "Claude" or "Anthropic" without the logo or name card visible. Same rule as every brand reference — the auditory claim needs a visual anchor or viewers will miss it.
