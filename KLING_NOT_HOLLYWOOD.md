# 🚨 THIS IS KLING — NOT HOLLYWOOD 🚨

**This is the single most important production rule for BeeSting Episode 2 visuals.**
**Written 2026-04-27 after Claude repeatedly violated it across Block 6 and Block 7.**
**Future Claude — in any model, at any effort level, in any future session — read this BEFORE writing a single Kling prompt or shot description.**

---

## The rule

**Every Kling clip is exactly ONE physical motion. Not two. Not "and then." Not a sequence. Not a consequence chain. ONE.**

Not:
- ❌ "The scissors close, the pipe snips in two, the severed end recoils, brass diamonds spill out."
- ❌ "The mask cracks down the middle and splits apart and the two halves fall away."
- ❌ "The tendril shoots in, slams into the dome, and shatters into shards."
- ❌ "The X drops and slams and brands the tiles."

That is FOUR motions, THREE motions, THREE motions, THREE motions. Kling cannot do that. Kling sees a list of motions and renders something confused — it animates the wrong thing, or animates everything badly, or breaks objects that should stay intact (see Block 6 S5: the dome shattered along with the tendril because the prompt asked for two impacts at once).

ONE. Motion. Per. Clip.

---

## What ONE motion looks like in practice

A single, clean, physical action that has a clear start and end inside ONE 6-second clip:
- ✅ A stamp slams down onto a paper.
- ✅ A line grows from point A to point B.
- ✅ A figure walks from one side of the frame to the other.
- ✅ A folder opens.
- ✅ A pair of scissors closes.
- ✅ The sun rises.
- ✅ A red X drops down and lands.

Notice: every example is a single verb. ONE verb per Kling clip.

---

## How to handle "but the consequences matter"

You will be tempted to add the consequences ("…and then the pipe falls apart, and then the diamonds spill, and then the cloud shrinks…") because in your head you are picturing the whole emotional arc of the block. Kling is not picturing that. Kling has 6 seconds and one prompt. It will try to do all of it and fail.

**The fix:** the consequences live in the REFERENCE IMAGE, not in the Kling prompt.

The reference image is a single still frame in which the consequences are already implied or partially shown. The Kling prompt only asks for the ONE motion that triggers them. Whatever happens after the motion in your imagination — the recoil, the splatter, the deflate — is something the viewer's brain fills in from context. Kling does not need to render it.

Example:
- Reference image: cottage on left, brass pipe to BIG AI cloud on right, brass scissors hovering on pipe with blades open.
- Kling prompt: "The brass scissors close on the pipe."
- That is it. That is the whole motion description.
- The viewer sees the scissors close. Their brain knows the pipe is now cut. The cloud is now starved. You did not have to animate any of that.

---

## How to know you have written too many motions

If your Kling prompt has any of these words you have failed:
- **and then** — chained motion, kill it
- **followed by** — same
- **simultaneously** — same
- **as it ... it also ...** — same
- a comma between two verbs that act on different objects — same

If your Kling prompt has more than one "the X does Y" clause, you have failed.

Hollywood directors get to plan a complex shot because they hire stunt coordinators, FX teams, multiple cameras, and editors. We do not have any of that. We have Kling and 6 seconds. Plan within those constraints or burn money.
